# 2. Deep chemical basis: the ternary ZrB2–SiC–TaB2 system

## Functional purpose

The ternary system based on ZrB2, SiC and TaB2 is proposed as a reactive matrix capable of generating a protective skin under extreme conditions. The idea is not only to support the component, but also to make the surface react in a controlled way to the thermal and chemical environment.

## Motivation: why TaB2 is needed

Pure borosilicate glass formed from SiC and ZrB2 provides initial sealing, but above 1600 °C the glass becomes unstable. B2O3 volatilizes rapidly and the low-viscosity silicate phase can be stripped by aerodynamic shear. In this regime, TaB2 is introduced as a stabilizing secondary phase.

### Key TaB2 effects

- increases the viscosity of the oxide/glass phase;
- reduces oxygen mobility and diffusion into the bulk;
- improves the stability of the vitrified film above 1800 °C;
- suppresses B2O3 volatilization by binding boron in a higher-melting matrix.

## Component architecture: reactive skin and graded core

The ternary system is intended not only as a load-bearing matrix, but as a reactive skin that adapts to the thermal-chemical environment. It is therefore well suited for a component architecture with:

- a dense, glass-forming outer skin;
- a graded transition region with hybrid porosity;
- a supporting inner core or lattice.

## Chemical protection mechanism

At intermediate temperatures, the matrix reacts with oxygen to develop protective phases and seal cracks.

The conceptual reactions are:

- SiC + O2 → SiO2 + CO
- ZrB2 + O2 → ZrO2 + B2O3
- TaB2 + O2 → Ta2O5 + B2O3

These reactions provide a liquid borosilicate phase that flows by capillarity into microcracks and seals them effectively. The Ta2O5 species then modifies that phase to increase high-temperature durability.

## High-temperature stabilization: Ta2O5-modified glass

The Ta2O5 produced in situ dissolves into the silicate/bosilicate melt, altering its structure and improving its high-temperature behavior. This modified vitreous film:

- has higher kinetic viscosity;
- resists aerodynamic drag and surface erosion;
- reduces the inward diffusion of oxidizing species;
- remains coherent at temperatures where pure borosilicate would fail.

This makes the protective skin more robust in hypersonic flows and aggressive re-entry environments.

## Thermal cycle mechanism: ZrO2 transformation toughening

During cooling, ZrO2 can transform from tetragonal (t) to monoclinic (m) phase around 1170 °C. This transformation is accompanied by a volumetric expansion of roughly 3–5 %.

The resulting compressive stresses around ZrO2 particles can:

- choke internal microcracks;
- increase local fracture resistance;
- act as a transformation toughening mechanism similar to stabilized zirconia ceramics.

## Design takeaways

From an engineering perspective, this system offers a structured set of advantages:

1. active protection through controlled oxidation;
2. sealing of cracks with a liquid borosilicate phase;
3. high-temperature film stability provided by Ta2O5 modification;
4. transformation-toughening support from ZrO2 phase change.

These properties become especially interesting when integrated with an aperiodic geometric architecture and a 3D interlock weave.
