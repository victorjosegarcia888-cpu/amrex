# 3. Hybrid manufacturing process

## General idea

To move from a fiber preform to a functional monolith, it is necessary to combine several manufacturing routes. The goal is to produce a structure with a dense protective skin and a porous support lattice or core.

## Process stages

### Step 1. Automated weaving of the 3D preform

A fiber preform is manufactured with continuity in several directions. The geometry is defined in a controlled way so that the interlacing is compatible with the structural logic of the component.

### Step 2. Infiltration of the ultra-refractory phase

A suspension of ZrB2 and TaB2 nanoparticles is introduced into the pores of the weave. Infiltration can be assisted by vacuum, pressure, or sol-gel techniques to ensure uniform penetration. The process can also be extended with:

- **PIP (polymer infiltration and pyrolysis)** for fine pore filling in the outer skin;
- **CVI (chemical vapor infiltration)** to deposit SiC on internal fiber surfaces and densify the matrix;
- **colloidal suspension control** with zeta potential adjustment to improve particle wetting.

### Step 3. Consolidation by CVI

The material is subjected to chemical vapor infiltration to deposit silicon carbide on the internal surfaces. This stage densifies the matrix and improves continuity between fibers and the ceramic phase.

### Step 4. Optional pyrolysis or PIP

To close the remaining porosity in the outer skin, a ceramic precursor polymer may be added and later converted into dense ceramic after pyrolysis. This sequence is particularly valuable for the skin region, where high density and low permeability are required.

## Manufacturing zonation

Manufacturing can be adapted to the zone of the component:

- nozzle throat: higher density and greater mechanical resistance;
- transition zones: greater controlled porosity to manage thermal gradients;
- re-entry shield: dense outer skin and high-emissivity surface.

## Connection with quasicrystal geometry

The aperiodic form can be incorporated into the preform and into the density distribution. In this way, the process does not only manufacture a material, but a complete material architecture.
