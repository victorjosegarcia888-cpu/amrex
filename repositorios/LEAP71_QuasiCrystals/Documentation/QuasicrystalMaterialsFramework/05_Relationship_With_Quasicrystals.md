# 5. Relationship with quasicrystals and aperiodic patterns

## Geometric hypothesis

Quasicrystals and Penrose patterns offer a geometry that is not periodic, but highly ordered. This condition is especially interesting for architectures that must resist multiple load modes without developing a simple recurring failure pattern.

## Connection with material architecture

The relationship between quasicrystals and extreme materials can be understood at three levels:

1. Geometric: the aperiodic structure avoids trivial symmetries and distributes stresses better.
2. Structural: the weave can inspire a 3D interlock with multiple load paths.
3. Functional: the porous or dense distribution can be adapted to thermal and chemical criteria.

## Conceptual application in this library

The current library already allows generating 2D patterns and 3D quasicrystals, as well as converting them into wireframes and voxels. This base is useful for future explorations in which the material architecture is defined over a computationally generated quasicrystal volume.

## Direction for future work

A next stage could consist of:

- parameterizing densities by zone;
- introducing an outer skin and a porous core;
- assigning thermal and chemical properties to regions of the voxel field;
- generating input files for simulation or additive manufacturing.
