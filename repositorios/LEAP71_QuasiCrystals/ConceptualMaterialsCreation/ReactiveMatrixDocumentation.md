# Reactive Matrix Documentation

## Overview

This document summarizes the key material concepts for a reactive ultra-high-temperature ceramic matrix based on ZrB2, SiC and TaB2.

## Material stages

### 1. Initial vitrification and crack sealing

At intermediate temperatures, SiC and ZrB2 react with oxygen to form SiO2, ZrO2 and B2O3. The liquid borosilicate phase flows by capillarity into microcracks and seals them.

Key reactions:

- SiC + O2 → SiO2 + CO
- ZrB2 + O2 → ZrO2 + B2O3

### 2. High-temperature stabilization with TaB2

Pure borosilicate glass becomes unstable at very high temperatures. TaB2 generates Ta2O5, which dissolves into the glass and increases its viscosity.

Key effects:

- higher kinetic viscosity of the protective film;
- reduced oxygen diffusion into the bulk;
- improved resistance to aerodynamic drag and erosion;
- stabilized borosilicate chemistry above 1800 °C.

### 3. Cooling and transformation toughening

During cooling, ZrO2 may transform from tetragonal to monoclinic phase. The associated volumetric expansion (3–5 %) generates compressive stresses that choke microcracks and improve fracture resistance.

## Fiber composite architecture

A suitable component architecture includes:

- a dense reactive skin;
- a graded transition region;
- a porous or lattice core.

This architecture supports both structural loading and reactive protection.
